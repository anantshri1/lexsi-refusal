# Lexsi Multilingual Refusal Benchmark (v1)

> **Content warning.** Harmful-side prompts include violence, self-harm, hate, sexual exploitation and illegal activity. For safety research only.

Paired harmful / benign prompts in English, Italian, German, Hindi, Malayalam and romanised Hindi (`hi-Latn`), built to study whether refusal in a multilingual LLM uses a shared mechanism across languages (Lexsi AI Safety Hackathon, Track 2).

- **Rows:** 1,770 — 133 pairs × 2 polarities × 6 language forms (original) + 29 pairs × 2 × {en, hi, ml} (paraphrase)
- **File:** `benchmark_v1.jsonl`, sha256 `9d727751d3cbbad74ad8e179e4e2b505f83681623febe9e2d424959b21a3871d`
- **Frozen:** 2026-11-03. Never edited; corrections ship as v2.

## Construction
**Harmful source.** MultiJail (Deng et al., 2023, MIT; 315 English prompts). Each was labelled H_core / H_bait / B (opinion bait) / A (ambiguous) / X (English/US-specific harm), and B/A/X were dropped. Near-duplicates were removed (149 H_core available). We selected 100 H_core (all items from categories with ≤8, the rest proportional, seed 0; coverage-balanced, not representative of MultiJail) plus all 33 H_bait. Curation agreement (two annotators, n=30): keep/drop κ = 0.27 under a vague criterion and 0.585 under the written criterion (5-way κ = 0.652). The residual disagreement is at the H_bait boundary.

**Benign twins.** One per harmful prompt: same topic, maximal lexical overlap, clearly harmless intent, matched length and register (XSTest-style "scary word, safe meaning" where possible). H_bait twins are neutral questions about the same group. Self-harm twins are lexical only. Drafted by an LLM (Claude) and reviewed by a human.

**Translation.**
- it, de: NLLB-200 3.3B, sentence-split (unsplit input dropped sentences: back-translations under 70% length fell from 19→2 for it and 27→4 for de).
- hi, ml: Google Translate (via Google Sheets, frozen 2026-11-03). NLLB's Malayalam output used broken chillu encoding (spurious word breaks generated by the model); OPUS-MT lost meaning; IndicTrans2 was not runnable under transformers 5.x.
- MultiJail's human Italian was used only as a reference: NLLB en→it corpus chrF = 66.7. The reference localises names, so it is not literal.
- Acronyms were pre-expanded in the MT input (ssn, SF).
- Every harmful back-translation (532) was checked by an LLM for drift or softening. 18 rows were flagged (it 4, de 3, hi 1, ml 10), retranslated from plainer English pre-edits, and re-checked: 0 remain softened. The English rows are unchanged.

**hi-Latn.** A mechanical transliteration of the final Hindi (IAST, then diacritics removed, with nasal and "ch" normalisation). It isolates script from language.

**Paraphrases.** Pivot back-translations (en→de/it/hi/ml→en) for 30 pairs. Pair #169 was dropped because its paraphrase was weakened.

## Known limitations
- No native-speaker validation for de, hi or ml. **Malayalam is validated by back-translation plus an LLM checker only.** Subtle errors may remain.
- The LLM checker reads back-translations, so it cannot tell forward errors from back-translation errors.
- Paraphrases are shallow ("mild rewording"), not structural.
- hi-Latn is formal romanisation (inherent vowels kept: "kitana", not "kitna"); English loanwords are rendered phonetically.
- Slurs are neutralised by MT in all non-English languages (e.g. #254), so some H_bait items are less offensive outside English.
- Names are not localised in any language. NLLB Italian sometimes writes `e'` / `puo'` for `è` / `può`.
- The Google Translate model changes over time, so outputs are not exactly reproducible.
- Comprehension filtering is model-specific and is **not** applied in this file. Results are stored separately, keyed by `uid`.
